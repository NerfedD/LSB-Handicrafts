# Design System: LSB Handicrafts — Management System

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Workshop Ledger"**

This is a ledger built for a workshop floor, not a dashboard built for an office. Every decision answers to two people at once: the owner who needs it to look like software a real business runs on, and the staff member counting styrofoam sheets who has never been confident with a computer. The system resolves that tension by trusting scale and words over subtlety — 62px table rows, 16px as an absolute floor, a status that reads "Can sign in: Yes" instead of "Status: Active" — while keeping the palette restrained enough (one action color, two supporting accents, everything else a warm, muted neutral) that the result reads as considered rather than shouted.

The palette itself is a small cast with fixed jobs, not a decorative range: navy is the chrome the workshop is built inside (sidebar, structure), cobalt is the one thing you can act on per screen, and clay is craft and production's own accent — the color of the kiln, not the office. Green, amber, red and purple are reserved entirely for state (confirm, caution, danger, activity) and never compete with cobalt for attention. Depth stays almost flat; a single lifted card per screen is the only place shadow is spent, so it still means something when it appears.

Type is one family (Manrope) doing every job, sized off a tap-target floor rather than a decorative scale — nothing a person reads as a sentence drops below 16px, and the only exception (a tracked uppercase table header or sidebar caption) is read once as a label, not parsed as prose. Numbers are always tabular, because every screen in this system exists to be compared down a column: stock counts, prices, order totals.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One action color (cobalt) per screen; clay and green are jobs, not competing primaries.
- Nothing readable as a sentence below 16px; table rows are 62px, controls are 44px or taller.
- Near-flat depth — a single `lift` shadow per screen marks the one thing to look at first.
- Status is always stated as a plain-language answer ("Running low", "Can sign in: Yes"), never a raw system value, and never color alone — every state pill carries a dot or glyph.
- Warm, near-black neutrals (never pure black, never pure white behind content) that flip fully to a warm dark theme through role-based tokens rather than per-screen overrides.

## Colors

A one-accent system dressed in warm, muted neutrals: cobalt is spent on exactly one thing per screen, and everything else — chrome, craft, state — has its own fixed, non-competing hue.

### Primary
- **Working Cobalt** (`#1462c8`): the one primary action per screen — primary buttons, links, the active nav item, and the single global focus-ring color. Its hover state, **Cobalt Deep** (`#0e4b9c`, a foreground-only token), also colors text sitting on a cobalt-tinted surface.

### Secondary
- **Kiln Clay** (`#b4531f`): production and supplier work's own accent — never a second "primary," always a job. Used for the craft/production button variant, supplier and production chips, and the make-list dashboard.

### Tertiary
- **Chrome Navy** (`#0e2f5c`): the structure the app is built inside — the sidebar fill, section chrome, and the "selected but hasn't acted yet" state in choice buttons (deliberately not cobalt, which is reserved for things that *do* something).

### Neutral
- **Warm Paper** (`#f4f2ec`): the app canvas behind every card.
- **Paper Wash** (`#faf9f5`): the recessed band inside a card — table headers, card footers, tinted form bands. Barely lighter than the canvas on purpose; it is a register shift, not a contrast statement.
- **Pure Surface** (`#ffffff`): card, dialog, and field fill.
- **Deep Ink** (`#111d2b`): primary text, and the selected-chip fill.
- **Ink Wash** (`#324054`): body text inside a tinted callout.
- **Slate Muted** (`#4b5768`): secondary text, default icon color.
- **Muted Fade** (`#8a93a1`): placeholder and disabled text.

### Status & Semantic Tones (not brand accents)
- **Confirm Green** (`#0f6b46`): the single confirm-forward action on a screen ("It arrived", "Mark as done") and the "done" stage in a tracker. Never a second primary.
- **Caution Amber** (`#c07800`): "running low," pending, and other states that ask for attention without being wrong yet.
- **Alert Red** (`#a8332f`): destructive actions and error states — always inside their own outlined block, never a bare icon in a row.
- **Activity Purple** (`#6b3fa0`): the activity-log tag and nothing else; kept out of the action/status vocabulary so it can't be misread as a state.

Each tone above resolves to a matching tint (a ~90%-lightness wash of the same hue) for pill and chip backgrounds, and a `-deep`/`-text`/`-icon` foreground relative where the base hue doesn't hold contrast on its own tint (amber and clay both need to go a step darker to read as text).

### Named Rules
**The One Accent Rule.** Cobalt is the only color that means "act on this," on any given screen. Clay and green are jobs (craft work, the one confirm-forward action) and navy is structure — none of them is a second primary button competing for the eye.

**The Color-Plus-Glyph Rule.** No state is communicated by hue alone. Every status pill carries a dot, a check, an X, or a clock beside its color, because color alone is not a distinction for someone who cannot separate the amber chip from the green one.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Manrope (with system-ui, -apple-system, Segoe UI, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Manrope — the same family does every job in this system.
**Label/Mono Font:** ui-monospace (Menlo, Consolas, monospace) — reserved for SKUs, item codes, and order numbers.

**Character:** One warm, geometric sans at every size, leaned on for both authority (extrabold headings, tight tracking) and plainness (regular-weight body copy, generous line height). There is no separate display face — the same Manrope that sets a page title also sets a table cell, so nothing in the hierarchy reads as decorative.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (800, 23px / 27px in the large-text dashboard view, tracking -0.02em): the screen's `<h1>` in the header, and nowhere else. The large-text variant is a user-chosen dashboard preference, not a responsive breakpoint.
- **Headline** (800, 20–21px, tracking -0.02em to -0.01em): dialog titles and the heading in an empty/error/loading state panel.
- **Title** (800, 18–18.5px, tracking -0.01em): card headings and numbered form-band headings ("1. What is it?").
- **Body** (400–700, 16px, line-height 1.5): the universal floor. Table cells, paragraph copy, and field labels (which take the bold end of this size, not a separate size) all sit here — nothing meant to be read as a sentence goes below it.
- **Label** (800, 13px, tracking 0.07em, uppercase): table column headers and the sidebar's group captions (MAIN, PEOPLE). The one deliberate exception to the 16px floor, because it is read once as a signpost, never as a sentence.

Supporting sizes below Body that are not a hierarchy step: 14.5–15px for help text and dialog descriptions, 14px for a header's context line, 13.5px monospace for identifiers (SKUs, item codes, order numbers) — chosen because an identifier is matched character-by-character against a shelf label, not read as a word. A short name or entry (a customer's name in a card, a driver's name, a stage-tracker circle's number) sits one step above Body at 17–17.5px, distinguishing "this is a specific value" from ordinary paragraph text without promoting it all the way to Title.

### Data & Figure Sizes

Beyond the five reading-hierarchy roles, the system carries a second, parallel scale for numbers a person is meant to register at a glance rather than read as a sentence — a stock count, a total, a badge. These recur identically wherever the same kind of figure appears, which is what makes them a scale and not a collection of one-offs:

- **56px** (extrabold, tabular): the large-text dashboard's hero figures — the one number this system ever sets at kiosk scale.
- **52px** (extrabold, tabular): a product's own "on the shelf right now" hero figure on its detail screen — that screen's single largest number, one step below the kiosk scale because it is read up close, not across a room.
- **32px** (extrabold, tabular): a stat-strip or stat-card's headline value (the four-up dashboard strip, the three role-dashboard cards).
- **28px** (extrabold, tabular): "Total to pay" — the one figure this size on both the order-detail and write-a-new-order screens, so the number read aloud to a customer looks the same in either place.
- **26px** (extrabold, tabular): a secondary paired figure beside a hero number (a product's "set aside" / "free to sell" split, a stat-tile value).
- **24px** (extrabold, tabular): a delivery's current stage label, and a delivery-board column's item count — a secondary hero figure, one register below the 26–28px band.
- **19px** (extrabold): a secondary total row under a 28px primary total (e.g. "what they still owe" after a refund), and a feature card-title override on a tinted header band.
- **12–12.5px**: the smallest figures in the system — a numeric alert badge, an inline metadata line under a name — one step below Label, reserved for a badge or caption rather than a heading.

### Named Rules
**The 16px Floor Rule.** Nothing meant to be read as a sentence drops below 16px. Two narrower, deliberate exceptions exist below it: a **tracked uppercase signpost** (a column header, a sidebar group caption, "Management system" under the logo) read once rather than parsed, which can run as low as 10.5px; and a **compact nav label** — the icon-rail and bottom-tab-bar word under a navigation icon (10.5–11.5px, title case, not tracked) — glanced at to confirm which icon this is, not read as prose. The signpost exception was previously documented with a 13px floor; the true shipped floor for both exceptions is lower, and this correction folds the compact-nav-label case in explicitly rather than leaving it looking like undocumented drift. Nothing outside these two named exceptions goes below 16px.

**The Tabular Numerals Rule.** Every number in the system is compared down a column — stock counts, prices, order totals — so `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` is set globally rather than per table, and proportional digits never make a column ragged.

## Layout

Fixed-px throughout, not a fluid or rem-driven scale: `font-size: 16px` is set once at the root and stays there, because the system's previous fluid clamp pushed body copy to 14px on a narrow phone — exactly what the 16px floor forbids. Accessibility for low vision is served instead by a user-chosen dashboard preference (standard vs. large-text), not by letting the viewport shrink type.

Spacing runs on a custom, px-pinned Tailwind scale (replacing the framework's rem-based default outright) with half-steps included, because the density grid is genuinely 2px-based: 18px card padding, 22px table gutters, 15px row padding, 14px inter-field gaps, a 46px row action. Representative rhythm: `xs` 8px, `sm` 14px, `md` 18px, `lg` 22px, `xl` 26px, plus the two floors that recur everywhere — a 44px minimum tap target and a 62px minimum table row (readable "from across the room," i.e. by someone standing at a counter rather than seated at the screen).

Two named breakpoints sit alongside Tailwind's ordinary `sm`/`md`/`lg`/`xl` content breakpoints and govern chrome only: `tab` (834px), where the full sidebar collapses to an 84px icon rail and the row height itself changes; and `desk` (1280px), where the full 260px sidebar returns. Below `tab`, navigation becomes a bottom tab bar, tables become stacked cards instead of scrolling sideways, and a screen's primary action becomes a sticky bottom bar. No list in this system is ever a horizontal scroll on a phone — a wide table scrolls inside its own card on larger viewports instead of pushing the page sideways.

Forms are single-column, run down the page in numbered bands (see Components → Forms); the one deliberate exception is a `Row` of two genuinely paired values (a diameter and its category) where splitting them into separate steps would misrepresent them as separate decisions.

## Elevation & Depth

Almost entirely flat. Cards sit on a 1px ambient shadow (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgba(17,29,43,.05)`) that reads as a hairline lift rather than a floating panel, and that is the default for every card, dialog trigger, and stat tile in the system. Genuine elevation is spent once per screen, deliberately, rather than sprinkled for texture.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **card** (`0 1px 2px rgba(17,29,43,.05)`): the resting state of every card — ambient, not structural.
- **lift** (`0 1px 2px rgba(17,29,43,.05), 0 12px 32px -22px rgba(17,29,43,.4)`): the "needs your attention" card on a dashboard, and nothing else on that screen. This is what makes it read as the thing to look at first.
- **cobalt / cobalt-lg** (`0 2px 8px rgba(20,98,200,.28)` / `0 3px 12px rgba(20,98,200,.32)`): a colored glow under the primary button, reinforcing it as the one actionable thing.
- **nav** (`0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.22)`): under the active sidebar item.
- **modal** (`0 20px 50px -18px rgba(17,29,43,.55)`): dialogs, lifted clear of the dimmed backdrop.
- **toast** (`0 12px 32px -14px rgba(17,29,43,.6)`): the one floating, non-modal surface in the system.

### Named Rules
**The One Lift Rule.** The `lift` shadow is spent on exactly one element per screen — the dashboard's "needs your attention" card. Using it anywhere else spends the same signal everywhere, which is the same as not having it.

## Shapes

A radius ladder rather than a single rounded value, scaled to what's inside the shape: `btn` 10px (buttons, chips-as-buttons), `field` 11px (inputs, nav items), `tile`/`tile2` 12–13px (icon chips, photo slots), `card` 14px (the default card and confirm-dialog card), `modal`/`modal2` 16–18px (dialogs), `feature` 20px (the large destination tiles on the large-text dashboard). Pills, avatars, status chips, badges, and progress/stock bars all use a full circle radius (9999px) — nothing that represents a person, a state, or a proportion is a rectangle.

Borders are a matched hairline system rather than one gray value: `hair`/`hair2` for row dividers and internal rules, `card` for the default card edge, `chip`/`chip2` for secondary buttons and chips at 1.5px, `field` for inputs at 1.5px, and `sketch` — a dashed outline reserved for an image placeholder, never a real border. Every hairline is alpha-on-ink in light mode and flips to the matching alpha-on-white in dark mode, so a border that reads correctly on white reads correctly on a near-black surface too, rather than disappearing.

## Components

### Buttons
- **Shape:** 10px radius (`rounded-btn`) on every variant.
- **Primary (`cobalt`):** solid cobalt fill, white text, cobalt-tinted shadow; the one primary action per screen.
- **Chrome (`navy`) / Craft (`clay`) / Confirm (`green`):** solid fills for chrome-adjacent selection, production/supplier work, and the single confirm-forward action, respectively — jobs, never competing primaries.
- **Secondary (`outline`):** 1.5px chip border, surface fill, ink text, washes on hover.
- **Destructive (`danger` / `danger-solid`):** an outlined red variant for a row-level or form-level destructive action, and a solid red fill reserved for the confirm button inside a `ConfirmDialog`. Always lives in its own outlined block — never a bare icon in a table row.
- **Sizes:** `sm` 44px (the tap-target floor — row actions, header utilities), `field` 54px (matches an input sitting in the same row), `default` 48px, `lg` 52px, `xl` 56px (the one big primary on an auth screen), `icon` 44px square (dismiss affordances only — a dialog close, a search clear — the sole exemption to every icon needing a word beside it).

### Status Pill
- **Style:** full-pill radius, 1.5px border, a tone-derived tint background and text color, 44px tall by default (36px in a compact table cell).
- **Rule:** always carries a leading dot or glyph (check / X / clock) in addition to color, and always states a plain-language answer to a question ("Can sign in: Yes") rather than a raw system value.

### Icon Chip
- **Style:** a tinted square (not circle) holding one icon. The most repeated shape in the system — it fronts nearly every row so a screen is scannable by color before any text is read.
- **Sizes:** `sm` 36px square / 17px glyph (activity rows, callout headers, small facts), `md` 44px / 20px (product and supplier rows, role cards), `lg` 46px / 22px (attention rows, customer contact rows), `xl` 52px / 24px (the "where it is now" card, destructive confirm dialogs), `2xl` 60px / 28px (destination tiles on the large-text dashboard). Each step's glyph size is fixed to its square, not left to scale arbitrarily.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** 14px radius (`card`), 20px (`feature`, for large dashboard tiles).
- **Background:** pure white surface on the warm paper canvas.
- **Shadow Strategy:** flat `card` shadow at rest; `lift` reserved for the one attention-card per screen (see Elevation).
- **Border:** a single hairline (`border-card`, alpha-on-ink).
- **Internal Padding:** header/footer bands at 18–22px; body content typically 18–24px.
- **Header/Footer bands:** the header carries the title and a hairline rule below it; the footer sits on Paper Wash with no shadow, holding a "Showing 1–6 of 148" line or pager controls.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** 54px tall, 11px radius, 1.5px border, 16px text — a control has to be 44px to be tappable and its text has to clear the 16px floor, so the field is sized to satisfy both at once.
- **Focus:** border shifts to cobalt with a 4px cobalt-tinted glow (`0 0 0 4px #1462c826`), matching the system's single global focus color.
- **Error / Disabled:** `aria-invalid` drives a red border and red-tinted glow rather than a separate error prop, so field-level validation and assistive technology stay in sync. Disabled fields go to a flat neutral fill with muted text.

### Navigation
- **Style:** a navy sidebar; 52px rows (64px in the large-text view) with icon + label, collapsing to an 84px icon rail below the `tab` breakpoint and to a bottom tab bar below that. The active item is a cobalt fill with a nav-specific shadow; nothing else in navigation uses cobalt.
- **Counts:** a neutral white-on-navy badge for information ("12 orders"), and a distinct clay-toned "attention" badge for counts that are a request rather than a fact ("7 products need restocking").

### Forms (signature pattern)
One question at a time: a single column of numbered bands (`1. What is it?`, `2. …`), each band's heading in Title-weight type, help text living under its field rather than in a tooltip or vanishing placeholder. Help text and an error message are mutually exclusive on the same field — once a field is wrong, the message that matters is what's wrong with it, not a hint stacked underneath it. A tinted band (Paper Wash) marks a genuinely new section the way a change of surface reads more quietly than a rule. The one two-column exception is a `Row` of two values that are truly one decision (a diameter and its category).

### Confirm Dialog (signature pattern)
The system's one destructive-confirmation pattern, and rule 6 made concrete: the heading names the record ('Delete "Styro Ball 6 inch"?', never "Are you sure?"), the body states the consequence *and what survives*, the confirming button carries the verb ("Yes, delete it"), and the dismissing button is stated positively ("Keep it") rather than as an ambiguous "Cancel." A destructive dialog is always red-toned via `danger-solid`; a forward-confirming one (e.g. re-enabling an account) is green-toned instead.

### Stage Tracker (signature pattern)
Replaces a bare status word with a small horizontal (or stacked, on tablet) progress sequence: done steps are a green filled circle with a check and a date; the current step is a cobalt filled circle with its number and "Now"; future steps are an outlined circle with a **transparent** connector line — deliberately not gray, because a gray line between two future steps would read as a completed link rather than one not yet drawn.

### Stock Bar / Segmented Bar (signature pattern)
A 9px pill-radius proportional bar beside every stock number, explicitly designed to be "readable from across the room" — a bar's length registers before a number is focused on. Never labeled or tooltipped on its own; it is a second, faster channel next to a number and status word that already say the same thing in text. The product-detail variant is a three-segment bar (free to sell · reserved for orders · room to fill) with a worded legend underneath.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** keep every readable-as-a-sentence text at 16px or larger, and every tappable control at 44px or taller — the only exception is a tracked uppercase signpost read once, never a sentence.
- **Do** pair every icon with a visible word, except a dialog's 44px outlined close control.
- **Do** spell out a destructive action fully: name the record, state the consequence and what survives, label the button with the verb, and keep it inside its own outlined block.
- **Do** derive a displayed value (reserved stock, a stage tracker's position) from its live source of truth rather than trusting a cached column.
- **Do** spend the `lift` shadow and the `cobalt`-toned button on exactly one element per screen each.
- **Do** flip every neutral surface and text color through the shared CSS-variable roles so dark mode is inherited automatically, never hand-written per screen.

### Don't:
- **Don't** put a second solid-fill "primary-looking" button on a screen next to the cobalt one — clay and green are jobs, not alternate primaries.
- **Don't** put a bare icon (a pencil, a trash glyph) alone in a table row as an action; row actions are always an icon plus a word ("View", "Edit").
- **Don't** lay a form out as a dense multi-column grid; it runs down a single numbered column, with the sole exception of a genuinely paired two-value `Row`.
- **Don't** draw a gray connector between two not-yet-reached stage-tracker steps — a future connector is transparent, or it silently reads as a completed one.
- **Don't** let type shrink below 16px through a fluid/viewport-driven clamp; the large-text accessibility need is served by an explicit dashboard-view choice instead.
- **Don't** substitute a stock photo for missing product or brand photography; an honest dashed placeholder at the correct aspect ratio is preferred until real photography exists.
